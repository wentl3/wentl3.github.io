How do you control ESD in an HPC office lab?

ESD / grounding / EPA

Short answer

Tie every conductive and dissipative item to one low-impedance common ground, and hold relative humidity in a controlled range.

Once the lab space exists, the next job is to keep static out of it. Here is the short version.

Why static builds up

  • When two different materials, such as plastic and rubber, touch and then separate quickly, electrons move: one surface turns positive, the other negative.
  • Handling materials, walking on carpet and fast fluid flow in pipes all do this.
  • At low relative humidity the moisture film on surfaces is gone and air insulates better. A person or object can then carry a high static voltage.
  • Semiconductor parts are sensitive to far lower voltages. The damage can be permanent, and a spark can also ignite vapors.

The design: one equipotential system

  • Connect all conductive and dissipative materials to a common ground, so no potential difference is left to discharge across.
  • Metal chassis and work surfaces are grounded directly, by welding or clamping.
  • ESD mats, anti-static flooring and ESD garments are coupled indirectly, together with static elimination devices.
  • Equipment that is not grounded, and high-impedance materials, are neutralized with ionizers that release positive and negative ions. They work best where air convects well.

The protected area (EPA)

  • An Electrostatic Protection Area, set up under ANSI/ESD S20.20 and IEC 61340-5-1, wraps a stable equipotential environment around ESD-sensitive items such as IC components and PCB modules.
  • Every piece of equipment, tool, packaging container and operator inside is connected to the common ground, from receipt through assembly, testing and packaging.
  • The network is built from low-impedance copper strips or flat wires. Every grounding point is inspected daily, wrist-strap and main-ground continuity first.
  • The static ground is kept separate from the power neutral and the lightning-protection ground (TN-S). Common grounding electrodes are kept at low impedance.
  • Non-ESD-compliant insulating containers, plastic packaging and personal belongings are prohibited inside and are labeled.
  • ESD-sensitive items arrive in certified anti-static packaging. Conductivity, seal integrity and condition are audited.

People

  • Before entering: certified grounding wrist strap, anti-static clothing, anti-static shoes or heel grounders.
  • At the door, a test portal checks that strap and footwear grounding resistance is within the allowed range. The same range is checked daily for body, footwear and floor.
  • Seated work uses a wrist strap. Standing work uses anti-static footwear on conductive flooring.
  • A wrist strap has a built-in current-limiting resistor, so static drains without a perceptible shock.
  • An insulator in a strong electrostatic field stays a safe distance from sensitive devices, or gets ion blowers.
  • Very sensitive parts (bare dies, high-speed switching MOSFETs, RF modules) call for anti-static gloves or finger cots made of conductive fibers.

Materials

  • Materials fall into three classes by resistance: conductive, dissipative and insulating.
  • Dissipative flooring keeps its surface resistance within a set range.
  • Highly insulating items, such as certain packaging films or plastic housings, are labeled and kept outside the EPA, or ionized.
  • Point scans with an electrostatic field meter find the sources. A source at a high-risk field level gets a buffer zone, or the eliminator is moved and its airflow adjusted.
  • One uncontrolled insulating object can trigger ESD even when every person and tool is grounded.

Where the liquid side meets static

  • A low-conductivity liquid, such as certain mineral oils or synthetic coolants, charges quickly in fast flow. The discharge energy can ignite flammable vapors or mists.
  • Pipes and tanks are metal or stably conductive composite, with a continuous grounding path tied into the ESD network.
  • Flow ramps up and down gradually, by limiting the maximum Reynolds number and the pressure loss.
  • A set dwell time before startup lets charge dissipate. Relaxation time is calculated from each liquid's dielectric constant and conductivity.

Carts and racks

Shelving, carts, pallets and racks that are ungrounded or insulating become isolated islands. Tie them into the grounding network with anti-static mats and detachable grounding leads, so the whole flow from receipt to storage stays equipotential.
